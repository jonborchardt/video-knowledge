## Is the economy booming, or are paychecks falling behind? [heading:hook fp=4bc7f8f8e7]

Output kept rising through the first half of 2026, yet an hour of average pay bought less in September than it had a year earlier. ## What GDP, the jobs report and real pay show [heading:investigation fp=e1a682c364]

Start with output, the measure behind most talk of a boom. [s06 fp=1046f52428] In its third estimate, the Bureau of Economic Analysis put real GDP growth at an annual rate of 2.2 percent for April to June 2026, after a revised 2.5 percent for January to March. [s07 ok fp=4bcab022f1] In their September projections, the median Federal Reserve policymaker expected 2.3 percent growth for 2026 as a whole and put the economy's longer-run growth rate at 2.0 percent. ## So is the economy booming? [heading:answer fp=cb995f4748]

Not by these measures: output grew a little faster than the Fed's long-run pace in the first half of the year, unemployment is low and steady, and payrolls changed little in September. [s24 ok fp=7d61c0098c]

**qualification (inflation-2026/strong-employment-gdp-growth-can-coexist):** Growth and shrinking real pay did run side by side in 2026, but the jobs half of the picture was steady rather than strong, so this was output growth, not a hiring boom, alongside falling purchasing power. [s25 ok fp=c1c858e18e]

## How to test the next claim that the economy is booming [heading:takeaway fp=dcc3b63dc5]

When someone calls the economy booming, or broken, ask which measure they are reading. [s26 fp=746a84416f] Output, jobs and real pay are separate measurements, and this year they have pointed in different directions. [s27 fp=e8fb02ce1f]
