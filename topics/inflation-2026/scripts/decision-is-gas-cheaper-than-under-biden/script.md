## Is gas cheaper now than under Biden?

President Trump made the case at a cabinet meeting: "It was $4 and $5 for a gallon of gas", he said, "and now it's probably 225". [s04 ok] By this spring, CBS News was reporting pump prices "at their highest point since mid-2022". [s05 ok] Here is the record to check them against: the Energy Information Administration's national monthly average for regular gas, in dollars not adjusted for inflation. From February 2021 through January 2025, it came to $3.46 a gallon, with a peak of $4.93 in June 2022. [s07 ok] In 2025 the lowest month, December, came to $2.89, well above the president's guess. [s08 ok] Every month of 2025 was below the Biden-era average, but about level with the last months of his term. [s09 ok] Then the weekly price jumped, from $3.02 in the first week of March 2026 to $4.50 by mid-May. [s10 ok] News reports tied the jump to the war with Iran: MS NOW said gas was "up by well over a dollar since the war started", and NBC News put it plainly: "it's the war in Iran that's largely causing fuel prices to rise". [s11 ok] West Texas Intermediate crude oil jumped in the same month, from $64.51 a barrel in February to $91.38 in March. [s12 ok] Those reports tie the rise to the war; the price record shows when prices jumped, but on its own it cannot rule out other causes. [s13 ok] In October, President Trump made the same kind of claim about crude: "Oil was higher under Biden than it is under Trump". [s14 ok] In September, the latest month in the EIA's crude table, it averaged $97 a barrel, higher than in every month of Biden's term except March through July 2022. [s15 ok] Gas, meanwhile, averaged $4.36 a gallon in September. [s16 ok] Put the lines side by side and the pump price has moved with crude under both presidents: both peaked in June 2022, both ended 2025 lower than they started it, and both jumped in March 2026. [s17 ok] That fits what a guest on PBS NewsHour said, that the pump price "is set in a global market"; but prices moving together cannot show that policy played no part, since policy can move crude itself. [s18 ok] So whether gas is cheaper than under Biden depends on the month: yes, against his term's average, through February 2026; no, in every month since March, though still short of that 2022 peak. [s19 ok] But gas is one price among many. What about prices overall?

## Have prices risen less under Trump than under Biden?

The channel Squirrelly Economics put the comparison this way: "Prices rose about 21% over Biden's 4 years". [s21 ok] The Bureau of Labor Statistics' consumer price index agrees: it rose 21.4 percent from January 2021 to January 2025. [s22 ok] From January 2025 to September 2026, the latest month, it rose 6.1 percent. [s23 ok] But that sets a full term against one still under way, over a much shorter stretch. [s24 ok] Over stretches as long as this one so far, at each end of Biden's term, prices rose 13.5 percent at the start, January 2021 to September 2022, and 4.5 percent at the end, May 2023 to January 2025. [s25 ok] And the count is still running: the year-on-year rate climbed from 2.4 percent in February 2026 to 3.7 percent in September, the same months gas jumped. [s26 ok] So prices have risen more slowly this term than across Biden's term as a whole, though faster than in his last stretch, and by a smaller margin than the totals suggest; and the index cannot say how much of either rise any president caused. [s27 ok]

## Close

Both answers turned on the calendar: which month counts as now, and how long a stretch you measure. So when someone tells you prices are lower, ask: compared with when, and over how long? And when the pump price moves, check what crude oil did that month.
