## Is there inflation, and is it high? [heading:hook fp=93dc1874c3]

Speaking at a Montana rally in October, President Trump said "we have very little inflation". [s01 ok fp=10fa036a54] A CBS News politics reporter answered with another yardstick: "inflation is higher than when he took office". [s02 ok fp=f470e064ae] Both sides can point at the same government price index, so the quarrel is less about the data than about what each side measures it against. [s03 fp=1848ad0ebc] This article takes those comparisons in turn, then asks what is pushing prices up this year. [s04 fp=acad57d29c]

## How high is US inflation right now, compared with the target, the past and the 2022 peak? [heading:question-1 fp=469bb9ae2e]

The flattest version of the low-inflation case is Trump's own: "we don't have inflation". [s05 ok fp=b64b522897] The Bureau of Labor Statistics' September report contradicts it: the consumer price index for all urban consumers rose 0.6 percent in September, seasonally adjusted, and 3.7 percent over 12 months. [s06 ok fp=a24d083e10]

| | Yearly change |
|---|---|
| January 2020, before the pandemic | 2.5% |
| June 2022, the peak of the series | 9.1% |
| March 2024, the highest month of 2024 and 2025 | 3.5% |
| January 2025, when the administration took office | 3.0% |
| April 2025, the lowest month since March 2021 | 2.3% |

| September 2026, the latest reading | 3.7% |

Trump's own benchmark is the past: "we inherited the worst inflation in history", he told the same rally. [s07 ok fp=8818d2acad] The table cannot rank all of history, but it does show the peak he means: 9.1 percent in June 2022, against 3.7 percent now, less than half of it. [s08 ok fp=d06e9cee1e] Of January's reading, the YouTube channel The Right-Wing Populist said it was "the lowest we've had in five years". [s09 ok fp=aba94757d4] That was nearly right: since March 2021 only April 2025's 2.3 percent came in under January's 2.4 percent, which was close to the 2.5 percent of January 2020. [s10 ok fp=e0eee3b8ce]

**unresolved (inflation-2026/inflation-low-historical-standards-january-2026):** Whether that counts as low by historical standards depends on how much history you have: the government series cited here begins in 2020, so it can set January 2026 against the pandemic years but not against earlier decades. [s11 ok fp=ce9de4c11a] Within that record, calling January low was fair, but the verdict rests on this short span and is held with moderate confidence. [s12 ok fp=eba6cbafa3]

By September that description no longer fitted: 3.7 percent was above every month of 2024 and 2025. [s13 ok fp=b4ba69dbfa] Measured from January 2025, the month the administration took office, the rate has risen from 3.0 percent, which is the comparison the CBS reporter was making. [s14 ok fp=d88d162fa5] Trump also offers a shorter window: "we're at 1.7% inflation for the last three months", he told reporters. [s15 ok fp=9c0fcdad19] The seasonally adjusted index does not bear that out: it climbed from 332.568 in June to 336.298 in September, a pace of about 4.6 percent a year. [s16 ok fp=17b0ac2489] Move the window back to end in August and the pace drops to about 0.2 percent a year, because the index fell 0.4 percent in June. [s17 ok fp=c04a198782]

Figure decision-how-high-is-inflation/sc13: The seasonally adjusted price index around the summer of 2026: the June dip makes a window ending in August look flat. [decision-how-high-is-inflation/sc13 fp=64d3276ec8]

At its September press conference the Federal Reserve said "for more than five years, inflation has been running above target". [s18 ok fp=36d74e46a7] The Committee's September statement says inflation remains elevated against its 2 percent goal. [s19 ok fp=72a682e5db] Its participants project 3.7 percent for 2026 on PCE, the price index for personal consumption expenditures, against a longer-run rate of 2.0 percent. [s20 ok fp=088b61f20f] The statement and the projection do not cover earlier years; the consumer price index does, and its yearly rate has been at least 2.3 percent in every month with a reading since March 2021, though the Fed's goal is set on a different index, PCE, so this record shows the trend rather than the target's own measure. [s21 ok fp=56b370a244] A slower rate is also not the same thing as lower prices. [s22 fp=70571ff53f] Even The Right-Wing Populist, which welcomed January's figure, said "prices are technically expanding, but the actual rate is decreasing". [s23 ok fp=8530e894b9] The record after the peak shows it: from January 2023 to September 2024 the yearly rate fell from 6.4 percent to 2.4 percent, while the index itself rose from 299.17 to 315.301, about 5.4 percent. [s24 ok fp=0f650c3f49]

## What is pushing US inflation up in 2026: gasoline, tariffs, or prices across the board? [heading:question-2 fp=d5fdc85850]

The yearly rate eased from 3.0 percent in September 2025 to 2.4 percent in January and February 2026, then climbed to 4.2 percent by May. [s25 ok fp=84628a1f49]

[s27 ok fp=4ae92ae892] Gasoline jumped: its index rose 24.9 percent in March, 11.1 percent in April and 8.6 percent in May, then fell 9.7 percent in June, though the tables do not say how much of the overall rise that explains. Others say the rise is wider than fuel; an economist on PBS NewsHour said "the war in Iran is not causing medical care, dental care, toys, apparel". [s30 ok fp=cf98f0174b]

**qualification (inflation-2026/price-increases-have-broadened-beyond-energy):** The report backs part of that: used cars and trucks, airline fares, medical care and apparel were among the indexes that rose in September. [s31 ok fp=a521ae645c] But core prices rose 0.3 percent on the month, the same as in August, so prices rose in more places without the monthly core rate speeding up, though the yearly core rate edged up from 2.4 to 2.6 percent. [s32 ok fp=21692d4a48]

Some economists put the underlying rate higher; in June, Michael Strain said "I think underlying inflation is around 3%". [s33 ok fp=5f762be9e8]

**qualification (inflation-2026/underlying-inflation-running-around-three-percent):** Core inflation has sat a little below that all year, between 2.4 and 2.9 percent, and it had slowed from 3.1 percent in July and August 2025 to 2.4 percent in August 2026 before rising to 2.6 percent in September. [s34 ok fp=83a8518795]

Housing still runs hot: the shelter index rose 3.2 percent over the year, faster than the 2.6 percent for core. [s35 ok fp=46ff1542dd] On tariffs, New York Fed economists estimate a cumulative contribution of around 2.9 percentage points to price changes for the goods in their sample by February 2026. [s36 ok fp=54d1b716d9]

**qualification (inflation-2026/tariffs-have-measurably-raised-core-inflation):** The authors say the estimate compares those goods with less-exposed ones, should be read as illustrative of the size of the increase for the goods studied, and so cannot say how much of core or overall inflation tariffs explain. [s37 ok fp=f6cf889278]

## How to read the next inflation claim [heading:close fp=683e12f554]

Most of the people quoted here are reading the same index; what they disagree about is the yardstick. [s39 fp=162e6e3bd3] When you hear that inflation is high or low, ask what it is being compared with, and over which months. [s40 fp=9f8e97a0c7] To judge whether a rise will outlast fuel prices, look at core rather than the headline, and remember that a falling rate still means prices that are going up. [s41 ok fp=aae63a3202]
