# How much faster does AI make programmers? What the trials actually measured [meta.headline fp=0368da1766]

Story `decision:how-much-faster` (ai-coding), revision 2. SEO title: How much faster does AI make programmers? [meta.seoTitle fp=730d3b38be]. Validation: **passed**, 0 blocking, 28 warnings, 39/39 assertions verified.

> Workplace trials of AI coding assistants estimated modest average gains, while a randomized trial found early-2025 tools slowed experienced developers on mature projects they knew well. [meta.dek ok fp=6caf4f0458]

Description: Trials of AI coding tools estimate modest average gains at work, and early-2025 tools slowed experienced developers in mature projects they knew. [meta.description ok fp=45d24d4eca]

## Ten times faster, or slower than before? [heading:hook fp=373c7a6420]

Claims about how much AI speeds up programming run from enormous multiples to an outright slowdown, and both ends of that range point to evidence of their own. [s01 fp=138bfe38ce] This article sets those claims beside the studies that measured programmers at work, and asks of each study who was measured, on what work, and with which tools. [s02 fp=6289c90e70]

## How much faster does AI actually make programmers? [heading:question-1 fp=4605346db9]

DHH, speaking on the Lex Fridman Podcast, described "that magical 10X, 100X, in a few rare cases, 1000X productivity boost" and tied it to working with the agents directly. [s03 ok fp=65780db6b5] At the other end, many videos and posts repeat a single trial's finding, which the channel Macro Lens put as "they were 19% slower than when they weren't". [s04 ok fp=bcbcf53cc2]

Field experiments at three companies, which followed almost five thousand software developers in their regular jobs and partly over years, estimated that an AI code suggestion tool raised their output by about 26 percent. [s05 ok fp=a92f9ad4f1] Its authors call that substantially smaller than the earlier lab result, and suggest why: some kinds of coding are less readily helped by the tool, and coding is only part of a developer's job. [s06 ok fp=0ede50d74b] Another trial put the gain in development speed attributable to AI at roughly 21 percent, an estimate that did not reach the usual threshold for statistical significance. [s07 ok fp=8c92b730ac]

Table t1: Measured change with AI coding tools, by study; the rows measure different things (output, development speed, task completion time, lab productivity) on different baselines [t1 fp=2b3c3dfee7]

| | Measured change |
|---|---|
| Field experiments at three companies: increase in output with an AI code suggestion tool | 26.08% |
| Trial of development speed: increase attributable to AI, not statistically significant | 21% |
| METR randomized trial, experienced open-source developers, early-2025 tools: increase in task completion time, a slowdown | 19% |
| Copilot lab trial, greenfield-style task: increase in productivity | 55.8% |

**unresolved (ai-coding/real-enterprise-codebases-yields-modest-average):** These estimates do not settle how much AI helps across workplaces: the field estimate carries a standard error of 10.3 percent, and the other trial's estimate missed statistical significance. [s09 ok fp=95d63569ce]

The slowdown figure comes from METR, which had 16 experienced open-source developers complete 246 tasks in mature projects where they had an average of 5 years of prior experience, with AI tools allowed or disallowed at random for each task. [s10 ok fp=cd29278353] The tools were the February to June 2025 frontier, chiefly the Cursor Pro editor with Claude 3.5 and 3.7 Sonnet. [s11 ok fp=d4fbad6555] When AI was allowed, tasks took 19 percent longer to complete. [s12 ok fp=00f098a243] METR's own hypothesis is that AI helped its developers less than earlier trials found because their projects were large and old, on average about 10 years old with more than 1,100,000 lines of code, where earlier trials used more greenfield projects. [s13 ok fp=b6c1f1e162]

Figure decision-how-much-faster/sc11: The average age and size of the projects in the METR trial, which the researchers offer as a hypothesis for the slowdown. [decision-how-much-faster/sc11 fp=c4ea288b77]

**qualification (ai-coding/measured-slowdown-mainly-artifact-older-models):** METR's 2026 update compares periods, not models: from conversations with participants, it believes developers are likely more sped up from AI tools in early 2026 than in early 2025, and it does not say how much of that is due to newer models. [s14 ok fp=92261e6845] Its newer raw results estimate that tasks took 18 percent less time for returning developers and 4 percent less for newly recruited ones, yet both confidence intervals reach plus 9 percent, which would be a slowdown. [s15 ok fp=492daa24fa] METR calls this only very weak evidence for the size of the change, because significantly more developers now choose not to take part since they do not wish to work without AI, which it says likely biases its estimate of the speedup downwards. [s16 ok fp=06e23d8759]

Figure decision-how-much-faster/sc14: METR's 2026 estimates of the cut in task time with AI, for returning and newly recruited developers. [decision-how-much-faster/sc14 fp=9cc406befe]

The largest figures in this evidence come from Anthropic's report on its own staff: employees self-reported a 50 percent productivity gain from Claude on average, and merged pull requests per engineer per day rose 67 percent when its engineering org adopted Claude Code. [s17 ok fp=5d6f2aa75b] A self-report and a count of merged changes are not measured time saved, and even read at face value both figures are an order of magnitude short of ten times. [s18 ok fp=1ef2ba124a]

**unresolved (gap 1):** The tenfold claim for agentic coding is unmeasured rather than disproved: the largest workplace estimate above is for a code suggestion tool, and Anthropic's figures are its staff's self-reports and a count of merged changes. [s19 ok fp=a86e751ed9]

**unresolved (gap 0):** A report on code migrations gives a larger saving in total migration time against a similar exercise without LLM assistance, but that estimate was reported by the engineers doing the migration and concerns migration work, so it cannot decide how much AI speeds up everyday development. [s20 ok fp=cc32833abc]

**unresolved (gap 2):** Why the trials split is not established: METR offers project size and complexity only as a hypothesis, and says the data from its newer experiment give an unreliable signal of the current effect of AI tools. [s21 ok fp=c2c713b9e7]

So, with the assistants they tested, the workplace studies estimated modest average gains, far below the multiples in the hype. [s22 ok fp=dc161010f7] Experienced developers in large, mature projects they knew well were slowed by early-2025 tools, and METR's newer data are too weak to say whether today's tools reverse that. [s23 ok fp=7b566a4a0d]

## Do developers know whether AI is actually making them faster? [heading:question-2 fp=e0b003f4d4]

Josh Anderson, discussing a Stanford study on his channel, said that people "estimating how their productivity was affected by AI is effectively a coin flip". [s24 ok fp=1d75669399]

Before starting, METR's developers forecast that allowing AI would cut their completion time by 24 percent; after finishing, they estimated it had cut it by 20 percent, in the trial that measured them taking longer. [s25 ok fp=d9ff55cc6f] Forecasters outside the trial were further off: experts in economics predicted completion times 39 percent shorter, and machine learning experts 38 percent shorter. [s26 ok fp=6edaf8e75c]

Figure decision-how-much-faster/sc21: Predicted cuts in completion time in the METR trial, from the developers before and after and from outside experts. [decision-how-much-faster/sc21 fp=0d4f6f392c]

An earlier randomized trial of Copilot, a lab task on a more greenfield project, went the other way: participants in both groups estimated a 35 percent productivity gain on average, against a measured 55.8 percent. [s27 ok fp=f959247f49]

Figure decision-how-much-faster/sc23: The Copilot lab trial: the measured productivity gain beside the participants' average estimate. [decision-how-much-faster/sc23 fp=713fda5413]

Across these trials, self-estimates missed in opposite directions: too high for experienced developers in mature projects they knew, too low on a fresh lab task. [s28 ok fp=3d36fc1b21]

**unresolved (gap 3):** For today's tools the question is open: METR's later update notes that some developers self-report very high speedups, recalls that its earlier study found such estimates can be quite unreliable, and adds that the true speedup could be much higher among the developers and tasks selected out of its experiment. [s29 ok fp=0e2a138f94]

## What to take from the trials [heading:close fp=80ef75d259]

Every figure here belongs to particular developers, doing particular work, with particular tools, so a claimed speedup is worth only as much as the measurement behind it. [s31 fp=7729dd9a51] To learn what an AI tool does for your own work, time comparable tasks with it and without it rather than going by how fast it feels. [s32 fp=ce974b71ea]
