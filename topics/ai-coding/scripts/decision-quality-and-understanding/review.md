## Resolved review findings

- s24 (s24.b) missing-qualification (raised against revision 1, addressed in revision 2): The answer rests on one trial, but nowhere does the script say it covered a small app over a short period. The package lists that limit. The authors also say they could see only short-term maintainability. This matters because the hook asks about changing the code 'next year'.
- s16 (s16.a) missing-qualification (raised against revision 1, addressed in revision 2): 'Many of the issues they introduced still survive' is the authors' wording. Their own figure is 22.7% of tracked issues, and only 7.5-14.8% of commits introduce any issue at all. A viewer hearing 'many' with no size will picture most of the code, which overstates the finding.
- s22 (s22.a) missing-qualification (raised against revision 1, addressed in revision 2): The package limit says this experiment's 69 participants were mostly students. The takeaway (s28-s29) applies the result to reviewing a colleague's code, so leaving out the population lets a viewer assume professional developers.
- s23 (s23.a) missing-qualification (raised against revision 1, addressed in revision 2): The passage shows two re-checks, not 'several'. In one of them (the interaction model) the effect weakened and was no longer significant (p = 0.05). 'Kept its direction every time' is literally true, but it suggests a confirmation the passage does not give.
- s26 overgeneralization (raised against revision 1, addressed in revision 2): 'Whether that turns into harder maintenance later is something none of this evidence tested' can be heard as saying no study tested later maintenance at all. That contradicts s24, where the Echoes trial did test it with other developers. What is untested is whether the same people's weaker understanding makes their own later changes harder, which is the scope the writer's note gives.
- s22 (s22.a) missing-qualification (raised against revision 2, addressed in revision 3): "That study's participants" comes straight after the crowd-worker trial (s32-s33), whose people were mostly freelance or professional developers, so a viewer can hear this as the same people. The package says this experiment's 69 participants were mostly students, and the takeaway (s28) applies the result to reviewing a colleague's code.
