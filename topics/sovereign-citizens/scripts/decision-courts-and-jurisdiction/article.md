# Can anything you say, sign or declare take you out of a court's power? [meta.headline fp=e41a222d8f]

Story `decision:courts-and-jurisdiction` (sovereign-citizens), revision 1. SEO title: Sovereign citizen courtroom tricks: what courts ruled [meta.seoTitle fp=45faef321e]. Validation: **passed**, 0 blocking, 0 warnings, 59/59 assertions verified.

> Courts in the United States, Canada and New Zealand have rejected the idea that their power over you depends on your consent, and a declared status as a state citizen or a Moor has fared no better in the rulings reviewed here. [meta.dek ok fp=b914ef5c66]

Description: Courts have rejected the claim that their power needs your consent. What the rulings say about UCC 1-308, flag fringe and the act of 1871. [meta.description ok fp=7fed5e3425]

## The defendant who told the judge he did not consent [heading:hook fp=ad216bdf86]

An Ohio defendant opened his trial by declaring that he reserved his rights "per UCC 1-308 without prejudice" and that "I give the Court no consent". [s01 ok fp=55d15d499c] The idea behind those words is that a court holds power over you only if you let it. [s02 fp=0244865eab] The idea is sold in several versions, and each leans on a real document: a section of the commercial code, the law of the flag, an old act of Congress, or the theory that statutes are contracts. [s03 fp=21db74842b] Below, each version is set against what its document actually says and what courts did when someone relied on it, followed by the related promise that declaring a new status puts you outside the law. [s04 fp=d6496af53d]

## Is there anything you can say, sign or point to that takes you out of a court's jurisdiction? [heading:question-1 fp=5cb0304e77]

Promoters describe jurisdiction as something you hand over: the channel Out of Babylon says "there are various jurisdictions that do not have jurisdiction over you unless you grant them that jurisdiction". [s05 ok fp=98f093a614] To withhold it, The Grown Academy tells viewers to "write UCC 1-308 next to your signature All Rights Reserved". [s06 ok fp=18a47430f2]

UCC 1-308 belongs to the Uniform Commercial Code, and its text protects a party that "performs, promises performance, or assents to performance" while expressly reserving its rights; "without prejudice" or "under protest" is enough wording to do that. [s07 ok fp=00965c8c82] The official comment gives the purpose as letting performance go on "along the lines contemplated by the contract despite a pending dispute", and the section says nothing about courts, police or criminal law. [s08 ok fp=8c2f7f94f3] The Ohio defendant was found guilty on all four charges, and the appeals court dealt with his code in a single line: "Any arguments related to the UCC are unavailing". [s09 ok fp=44c9bb0ec3]

Refusing consent out loud has done no better than writing it down. [s10 fp=769845f8aa] In United States v. Benabe, a federal appeals court held that whatever status a person claims, "that person is not beyond the jurisdiction of the courts", and that such theories "should be rejected summarily, however they are presented". [s11 ok fp=3a2d496aa0] Legal researchers Hobbs, Young and McIntyre describe three claimants who treated a court's authority as resting on their own consent; all three lost, and the authors give the reason: "no one needs to explicitly and affirmatively consent to the authority of a jurisdiction to be subject to it". [s12 ok fp=ff30501ce3]

The flag version is stated in a letter to the Mountain Democrat: "The fringe denotes Admiralty law's jurisdiction". [s13 ok fp=0b1347f84e] In McCann v. Greenway, a federal court that knew other courts had already called the argument frivolous examined the law of the flag anyway, "in the interest of killing this argument for good". [s14 ok fp=6c27f8e695] It quoted the Adjutant General of the Army, who wrote that a fringe "is not considered to be a part of the flag, and it is without heraldic significance". [s15 ok fp=8bafb8f102] And it set out the rule that decides the question: "Jurisdiction is a matter of law, statute, and constitution, not a child's game wherein one's power is magnified or diminished by the display of some magic talisman". [s16 ok fp=a428ef5bcf]

The 1871 version claims far more; the channel SYDLIS says "the act of 1871 that is exactly what changed america from a constitutional republic to a corporation". [s17 ok fp=1db6c055cb] The act is real, and in Metropolitan Railroad Co. v. District of Columbia the Supreme Court explained that it made the District of Columbia "a body corporate for municipal purposes", a local government able to make contracts and to sue and be sued. [s18 ok fp=5cdc619405] The Court called the District "a mere municipal corporation", said its sovereign power sits "in the government of the United States", and recorded that an act of 1874 abolished the government the 1871 act had created. [s19 ok fp=0a6d14189a]

The widest version treats every law as a private rulebook; Americans In Action says "these codes and statutes are only the bylaws of the corporation". [s20 ok fp=883f5028a3] In Bey v. Indiana, when a man told the Seventh Circuit that Indiana could not tax him without a contract between them, the court answered "We have repeatedly rejected such claims". [s21 ok fp=dd95722aa8] In New Zealand, a self-described freeman-on-the-land who said he had never consented to the Land Transport Act had his claim struck out as an abuse of process; the researchers who reviewed the case note that "Everyone who operates a vehicle on public roads must comply with legislation regulating the operation of vehicles". [s22 ok fp=1800c728dc]

So the answer is no: courts in the United States, Canada and New Zealand have each rejected the claim that a court's power over a person waits on that person's consent. [s24 ok fp=26ded632e6]

**unresolved (sovereign-citizens/courts-uniformly-reject-sovereign-citizen-arguments):** How uniformly courts reject these arguments is best documented in Canada: Netolitzky and Warman report that no appeal built on them has been granted leave by the Supreme Court of Canada, and conclude "These ideas are pseudolaw, and devoid of merit". [s25 ok fp=b7079a7632] A review of cases from Australia and New Zealand reports that "No courts accept these arguments, and no courts absolve the claimant (or defendant) from their legal obligations and responsibilities". [s26 ok fp=9b03cd99d5] For the United States this evidence holds no comparable count, only a run of rulings, which an Ohio appeals court summed up by saying such claims "continue to make their way to court and fail". [s27 ok fp=e406af930d]

## Can declaring a different status, as a state national, a Moor or a free man, put me outside the law? [heading:question-2 fp=57c0b2349a]

If no words in the courtroom work, promoters offer a new identity instead; Sovereign National describes citizenship as the status "which forces you into their statutes and codes and bylaws and we are not part of that". [s28 ok fp=29109aead0] Some sellers promise it works at the roadside too, with SPC University saying "when the officer runs your id he or she will see your status and has to let you go". [s29 ok fp=f92c0453a5]

In federal statute, a national of the United States means "a citizen of the United States", or a non-citizen who owes the country permanent allegiance, so every citizen is already a national. [s30 ok fp=c2dd8bbcd3] The non-citizen kind of national status comes with birth, for instance in an outlying possession, not from a declaration a person files. [s31 ok fp=ef149549d3]

**unresolved (sovereign-citizens/declaring-yourself-american-state-national-removes):** The court rulings here on declared state citizenship all come from federal tax litigation. [s32 ok fp=f89088f634] In those cases federal appeals courts rejected the argument that being a citizen of a state rather than of the United States puts a person beyond the federal courts, and the Second Circuit called it "barely worth a footnote". [s33 ok fp=263b9ca9b5] Because they are federal tax cases, they do not test whether the same declaration could take someone out of a state court's reach. [s34 ok fp=cf7f32cf1c]

The Moorish version rests on old treaties, and the Institute for Strategic Dialogue explains that "Moorish sovereigns cite that treaty to claim that Moors in the US have sovereign status, meaning they would be exempt from normal laws". [s35 ok fp=0ea87028ab] In Bey v. Indiana, a man who said that being a Moor made him a sovereign citizen asked the State of Indiana for an $11.5 billion refund. [s36 ok fp=ffbc038ae3]

Figure decision-courts-and-jurisdiction/sc33: The refund the plaintiff in Bey v. Indiana asked for; the court dismissed the suit and awarded nothing. [decision-courts-and-jurisdiction/sc33 fp=570c406ba3]

The Seventh Circuit said the treaty argument is made "without any basis in fact" and that Bey "is a U.S. citizen and therefore unlike foreign diplomats has no immunity from U.S. law". [s37 ok fp=88266a6636]

The common-law version is taught by the National Liberty Alliance, which says "the un-codified common law is the superior law of the people and the codified civil law is the special or inferior law of the government". [s38 ok fp=90ee45040b] Magna Carta, often cited alongside it, had been repealed by 1969 except for three provisions, and in 1905 the High Court of Australia said the claim that a law is invalid for not conforming to it "is not one for serious refutation". [s39 ok fp=c0d75db87d] Netolitzky and Warman report that the Supreme Court of Canada refused leave to applications relying on Magna Carta and historical English common law, which they read as an implicit rejection. [s40 ok fp=4aca3a8d39]

No court in this evidence accepted a declared status, whether citizenship of a state, Moorish nationality, or a free man's appeal to Magna Carta and the common law. [s43 ok fp=a078cbe223]

## Where a court's power comes from [heading:close fp=b991f8c2b9]

Both answers rest on one rule: a court's power comes from law, statute and constitution, and no status a person claims puts them beyond it. [s44 ok fp=b6a0700845] Anyone offered a form, a code for the signature line or a card for the glovebox can ask the seller for a ruling where it worked, because the rulings where it was tried are public. [s45 fp=d9ae313fd8]
