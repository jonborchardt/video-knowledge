CONSUMER FINANCIAL PROTECTION BUREAU | MAY 2022
Supervisory Highlights, Issue 26 (Spring 2022)

Government supervisory report summarizing findings from CFPB examinations of financial institutions, including auto loan servicers.

2.1.3 Overcharging for add-on products
When consumers buy a car, auto dealers and finance companies offer optional add-on products, including guaranteed asset protection (GAP) products that help pay off an auto loan if the car is totaled or stolen and the consumer owes more than the car's depreciated value. These products' potential benefits apply only for a specific period (such as four years after purchase or for the loan's term) and only under certain circumstances. Dealers and finance companies typically charge consumers the full add-on cost as a lump sum at loan origination, folded into the total financing agreement, so consumers keep paying for the product over the loan term even if it expires years earlier.

Examiners found servicers engaged in unfair practices by failing to request refunds from third-party administrators for the "unearned" (unused) portion of GAP product fees, and failing to apply refunds already owed, after a vehicle was repossessed and the GAP contract canceled -- at that point the consumer no longer had the vehicle the GAP product covered, so the product offered no further possible benefit. Servicers maintained policies to obtain these refunds but frequently failed to actually request them. When a repossessed vehicle was sold and left a deficiency balance (money still owed after the sale), the servicers' failure to request the GAP refund meant the deficiency balance included charges for the unused, financed GAP portion that should have been offset by a refund. Servicers then sent deficiency notices to consumers, and reported these inflated balances to third-party debt buyers, for amounts higher than what consumers actually owed. Consumers could not reasonably avoid this injury: they had no control over the servicer's refund-processing actions, and most did not know their contract entitled them to a refund at all. In response, the servicers remediated affected consumers and added controls to ensure add-on refunds are processed after repossession.

DERIVED COMPARISONS
- A GAP product's benefit is tied strictly to the loan and the vehicle it covers: once the vehicle is repossessed (or the loan otherwise ends early), the product has no further possible benefit, so any unused, prepaid portion of its cost becomes a refund owed back to the consumer rather than a cost the consumer should go on paying.
- Examiners found this failure pattern (not requesting the GAP refund, then including the unrefunded GAP cost in the deficiency balance sent to debt buyers) occurred even though servicers already had policies on the books requiring them to request such refunds -- a gap between stated policy and actual practice, not an absence of a refund policy.
