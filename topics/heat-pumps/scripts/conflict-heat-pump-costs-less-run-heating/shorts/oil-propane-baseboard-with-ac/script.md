# Oil, propane or electric heat, plus AC? A national model says 95 to 100 percent of homes save.

Story `conflict:heat-pump-costs-less-run-heating/shorts/oil-propane-baseboard-with-ac` (heat-pumps), revision 3. About 1.3 minutes (178 words at 150 wpm). Validation: **passed**, 0 blocking, 0 warnings, 4/4 assertions verified.

## Hook

95 to 100 percent: the share of homes heating mainly with oil, propane or electricity, and already running air conditioning, that a national study estimates would pay lower bills with a heat pump. [s01 ok] The Commission has looked at what that figure is, and what it is not.

## Finding

It is a model: the study simulated the bills of such homes across the country, so the figure is an estimate of what bills would be, not a reading from anyone's meter. [s03 ok] The range moves with the heat pump's efficiency, and so does the saving: the median it estimated was $300 to $650 a year, for those same homes and no others. [s04 ok] The air conditioning is not a detail: in the same model, homes on those fuels without it do worse, with only 73 to 86 percent coming out ahead. [s05 ok] A model, the Commission notes, is exact about what it assumes.

## Close

Whether metered homes in Minnesota and New England agree with the model, and whether oil holds the pattern, is the subject of the full inquiry, linked in the description below.

## Open issues

- **info** s01 (s01.a) omission (review): The study states that its bill results use winter 2021-22 energy prices and are 'very sensitive' to them: at 2019 prices the share of oil-heated homes with positive bill savings falls to 66 to 100 percent (propane 87 to 99 percent). The Short attributes the figure to the model, s06 says a model is exact about what it assumes, and the price year is not among the package's carried qualifications, so the sentence is accurate as spoken; but the 95 to 100 percent is the main case at one winter's prices, not a figure that holds at any price. Suggestion: No change needed for the Short; the full video is the place to say the figure moves with the price year, if an editor wants it said.
