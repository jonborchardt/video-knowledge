About DOE Home Energy Rebates

Department of Energy received $8.8 billion to develop two home energy rebate programs: the
Home Efficiency Rebate (HER) program and the Home Electrification and Appliance Rebate
(HEAR) program. Both rebate programs are administered by State Energy Offices (SEO) with the
goal of increasing energy efficiency, lowering utility bills, and reducing carbon emissions for low-
to moderate-income households.

This resource guide provides an overview of each state’s Home Energy Rebate program status and
multifamily affordable housing program details. Please note that information is subject to change
as state applications are approved by DOE and programs launch.

Below are Home Energy Rebate program details based on federal guidance. States may increase
the maximum Home Efficiency Rebates eligible households/units may receive and/or limit
measures eligible for Home Electrification and Appliance Rebates.

California

Administering Agency: California Energy Commission
Program Status: HEAR rebates available; HER application conditionally approved
State Allocation: $582M
Program Description: California has launched phase 1 of the HEAR program which provides heat
pump and electrification rebates for multifamily buildings and single-family households. Apply for
rebates here.
HER funding will be split between two programs: the Equitable Building Decarbonization Direct
Install Program (60% of funds; no-cost upgrades for low-income residents; expected to launch in
2026), and a pay-for-performance pathway (40% of funds; rebates based on actual measured
energy savings; launch date TBD).

Colorado

Administering Agency: Colorado Energy Office
Program Status: Limited HEAR rebates available; HER application approved
State Allocation: $140M
Program Description: Colorado has launched the HEAR program through the state's
Weatherization Assistance Program to provide home energy upgrades to low-income households
receiving WAP services. Rebates will be available to households not participating in WAP this year.
Colorado does not plan to make HEAR rebates available to new construction and will target the
program at single family households and smaller multifamily buildings (less than 50,000 sqft). The
state’s HER program will provide rebates for comprehensive energy efficiency upgrades for large
multifamily buildings (50,000 sqft or larger) and mobile/manufactured homes. Colorado expects
to launch this program early 2026.

Massachusetts

Administering Agency: Massachusetts Department of Energy Resources
Program Status: HEAR and HER applications approved
State Allocation: $146M
Program Description: Massachusetts rebate funding will leverage existing state and federal
opportunities, like Mass Save. DOER also plans to expand existing affordable housing
decarbonization programs by funding additional measures, targeting decarbonization in existing
income restricted multifamily units, leveraging multiple funding sources outside of HER, and
exceeding max rebate limits to achieve greater energy savings for low-income units.
The state’s HER program will target 1 to 4-unit households served by Municipal Light Plans who
aren’t served by Mass Save. Multifamily households will be targeted through HER through the
DOER Affordable Housing Decarbonization Grant program. These funds will augment DOER and
other state funds awarded to these projects to help close funding gaps.
HEAR funding will be delivered through the Mass Saves program, targeting households at or below
60% AMI and through a newly expanded program for households with incomes between 61% and
80% AMI.

Vermont

Administering Agency: Vermont Department of Public Service

Program Status: HER application approved; HEAR application conditionally approved

State Allocation: $59M

Program Description: Vermont plans to allocate rebate funding across three distinct programs. 1)
Vermont proposes committing all of the state’s HER funding and $10M in HEAR funding to the
Vermont Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) serving households at or below 80% AMI. 2) An
additional $10M from HEAR would be invested into an Affordable Multifamily Housing New
Construction program to offset the costs of energy efficient electric appliances and heating for
new construction projects where at least 50% of tenants are at or below 80% AMI. 3) Vermont
proposes allocating the remaining HEAR funding (just over $9M) to provide rebates for heat pumps
to moderate income households who are not eligible for WAP.

Eligible measures for HER funding include weatherization measures, air sealing, ventilation,
insulation, and heat pump installations. The state’s Department of Public Service has identified heat
pumps and heat pump water heaters as measures that will deliver the biggest savings to
customers. Electric service upgrades will be eligible for HEAR funds if the household is also
installing cold-climate heat pumps.
