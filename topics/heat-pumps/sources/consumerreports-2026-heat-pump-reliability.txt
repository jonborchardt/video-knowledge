Most Reliable Heat Pump Brands of 2026
Heat pumps aren't trouble-free, but our newest survey data on brands like Coleman and Trane can help thwart long-term headaches
By Mary H.J. Farrell
Updated by Kala Herh
Because heat pumps cool and heat a house, CR recommends looking for one that runs reliably no matter the season.
Using heat pumps can be one of the most efficient ways to cool and heat your home. Compared with other heating and cooling devices, they are eco-friendly and could save you money. In cooling mode, they work like air conditioners, extracting heat from the air inside and releasing it outside. In heating mode, they do the opposite, extracting whatever heat is present in outside air (even cold outside air) and moving it inside to heat your home.
Though they use electricity, heat pumps use it more efficiently than electric baseboard heat and don’t require direct consumption of fossil fuels like oil or natural gas. Depending on factors such as where you live and the cost of electricity, a heat pump may also save you money compared with other heating and air conditioning systems. But heat pumps aren’t trouble-free, as we learned from surveys of our members who installed 13,753 of these systems between 2009 and 2025.
In our latest analysis, we found that 29 percent of heat pumps broke or stopped working by the end of the eighth year of ownership, essentially halfway through their expected lifespan. That makes them one of the least reliable products we have ratings for. (Our 2025 survey doesn’t contain data on failure rates.)
That said, several heat pump brands still received favorable ratings and five were rated exceptional. This indicates that while heat pump reliability may still be poor, overall some brands do a better job of keeping their units working than others.
Most and Least Reliable Heat Pump Brands
We analyzed data from our members who own heat pumps to find the most reliable ones and those they would recommend to others. The 22 brands we rated include well-known companies like Carrier, Lennox, and Trane, which account for more than half of the heat pumps chosen by members, as well as lesser-known companies like Heil and Payne. In all, 11 brands earned favorable marks for predicted reliability in our most recent analysis, which estimates how likely a particular brand’s heat pump is to experience problems by the eighth year of ownership.
