DERIVED COMPARISONS (computed for this file from Parts 1 and 2; not an EIA product)

Method. Constants: 1 MMBtu = 293.07 kWh; residential natural gas heat content 1.036 MMBtu per Mcf (EIA approximate U.S. average).
  Gas price per MMBtu of fuel  = (gas $/Mcf) / 1.036
  Electricity price per MMBtu  = (cents/kWh) x 293.07 / 100
  Price ratio R                = electricity $/MMBtu / gas $/MMBtu (energy-content basis)
  Cost of delivered heat ($ per MMBtu of heat): gas furnace = gas $/MMBtu / AFUE; heat pump = electricity $/MMBtu / seasonal COP
  Break-even seasonal COP      = R x AFUE. A heat pump whose seasonal average COP is above this number heats more cheaply than
  the furnace at these average prices (ignores tiered or time-of-use rates, and backup resistance heat).
Caveat: EIA average prices are total revenue divided by volume, so they include fixed monthly customer charges. A household that keeps its gas
  connection for other appliances keeps paying the fixed charge, and its marginal gas price is below the average; one that disconnects saves it.
Prices used: 2024 annual electricity with 2024 annual gas (same year). "Latest" uses Jan-Jul 2026 YTD electricity with 2025 annual gas.

Example (U.S.): electricity 2024 = 16.48 c/kWh -> 16.48 x 293.07 / 100 = $48.30/MMBtu; gas 2024 = $14.50/Mcf -> 14.50 / 1.036 = $14.00/MMBtu;
  R = 48.30 / 14.00 = 3.45; break-even COP at AFUE 0.95 = 3.45 x 0.95 = 3.28; at AFUE 0.80 = 2.76.
